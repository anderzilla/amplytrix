# ADR-0041: Ethical persuasion, aggressive competition and commercial integrity

Status: accepted — 2026-08-01

## Context and problem

Amplytrix needs strong conversion, retention and differentiation to sustain product investment. Revenue-only optimization can reward confusion, involuntary payment, impaired exit, fabricated evidence or harmful engagement, increasing chargebacks, churn, LGPD/consumer-law exposure and brand damage. Competitor misconduct is not an acceptable benchmark.

Drivers are measurable user outcomes, commercial speed, informed autonomy, trustworthy evidence, portability, accessible UX, privacy, legal readiness and bounded experimentation.

## Alternatives

1. Optimize conversion case by case with reviewer judgment: fast, but inconsistent and difficult to audit.
2. Avoid persuasive/gamified commercial techniques: reduces manipulation risk but weakens legitimate discovery, activation and competition.
3. Permit aggressive, evidence-backed persuasion under explicit commercial-integrity rules, guardrail metrics and blocking review: more work and slower experiments, but aligns growth with durable value. **Selected.**

## Decision

Compete aggressively through superior product, real outcomes, speed, experience, clear value, documented comparison, transparent pricing, consented personalization, relevant upsell/cross-sell, factual urgency/scarcity, authorized social proof, legitimate guarantees/trials, responsible progress mechanics and value-based loyalty.

Prohibit lies; illegal, criminal, abusive or immoral practice; dark patterns; confirmshaming; fabricated users/activity/results/metrics/reviews; false urgency/scarcity/prices/discounts; hidden fees, renewal, trial conversion, limits or financial effects; deceptive controls/consent; exploitation of fear, professional insecurity, emotion, children or vulnerable groups; data overcollection/silent reuse; obstructed cancellation/downgrade/export/portability; silent price/plan changes; artificial dependency; punitive or casino-like gamification; and claims beyond verified product capability.

Social proof is real, authorized, verifiable, contextual and representative. Pricing discloses amount, period, fees/taxes where applicable, renewal, limits, overage, add-ons and cancellation. Experiments record hypothesis, segment, duration, primary and guardrail metrics, user/legal/privacy impact, evidence, success and stop conditions. They stop when revenue gains depend on deception, confusion, accidental conversion, involuntary charge, privacy/trust harm, chargeback or material complaint/cancellation increases.

Commercial features undergo Ethical Persuasion and Commercial Integrity review. BLOCKER/CRITICAL/HIGH block promotion; potential impairment of conscious choice requires human review.

## Consequences and impacts

Positive: clearer offers, more defensible differentiation, healthier retention, fewer disputes and stronger trust. Costs: review time, evidence collection, experiment instrumentation, legal/marketing/UX coordination and slower launch of uncertain claims. Engineering must support consent, auditability, configuration, cancellation/export, metric integrity and experiment stop controls. Data collection grows only with named purpose/legal basis and minimization. Security reviews targeting, fraud, proof authorization and metric tampering.

Financial risk remains that ethical guardrails reduce short-term conversion relative to deceptive competitors; expected long-term benefit is lower chargeback/support/churn and stronger LTV. Legal risk requires counsel before material pricing, vulnerable-group targeting, recurring billing, personal-data experimentation or regulated claims. This ADR is policy, not legal advice.

## Validation, reversibility and review

Validate through governance asset tests, feature templates, claim-to-evidence matrices, commercial/guardrail metric definitions, manual homologation evidence and relevant UX/accessibility/privacy/security tests. The policy is reversible only by a superseding architecture update and ADR; prohibitions cannot be silently waived. Revisit after applicable law/counsel changes, new market/geography, vulnerable audiences, material pricing model changes, repeated false positives, significant experiment harm or evidence that gates are ineffective.
